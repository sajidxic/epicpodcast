Distribution

Why your own channels can only take you so far.

Clips posted to your accounts mostly reach your existing followers. Growth slows, not because the show is not good, but because you are speaking to the same audience.

01 Your own feed is a closed room.

Posting to the same accounts reaches the same people. The limit is not the quality of the show; it is the size of the room.

02 A theme page opens it.

An account built around a subject rather than your show’s name — founder lessons, AI builders, early-stage investing — so platforms surface it to viewers who have never heard of you. The accounts are yours.

03 Every day, not every episode.

Thirty to ninety clips a month, one to three a day across X, LinkedIn, Shorts and Instagram, each cut for the platform it lands on and pointed back at the episode, the guest and the brand.

60–90 days to traction · posted manually, not scheduled in bulk · the accounts stay yours

Projection · not a client

What to expect in your first ninety days.

Epic Podcast is new, and we have not yet taken a venture show to day ninety. What follows is the pattern our team’s short-form work has established, applied to a weekly interview show with a theme page running alongside it. The figures come from our case studies. It is a projection rather than a promise.

What we commit to from week one
  • One hour a week of your time. You record; we handle research, editing, packaging and publishing.
  • Seventy-two hours from recording to release, covering the full episode, the YouTube cut, clips and show notes.
  • Eight to twelve clips an episode on the theme page, published daily across X, LinkedIn, Shorts and Instagram.
  • A weekly report on what went out, what held attention and what we are adjusting.
01Days 1–30 · Foundation
4 → 40

Four episodes released and around forty clips published. Early reach is typically modest, as platforms test new accounts with small audiences first. This month tells us which topics, guests and formats hold your audience’s attention.

What you’ll see: a consistent publishing cadence, and a first read on which subjects resonate.
02Days 31–60 · Refinement
First break

We build on what is working and retire what is not. This is often when the first breakout clip appears. On RozzJGen’s run, the first outsized video arrived in month three rather than month one, so a quieter second month is not a cause for concern.

What you’ll see: an early breakout clip, and the first inbound interest, with guests accepting more readily.
03Days 61–90 · Momentum
Compounding

Consistency begins to return. RozzJGen went from 255,889 views over eight months to 11.5M by month twelve, with most of that arriving in the second half. By this stage your audience has seen enough of you to begin trusting the work.

What you’ll see: returning viewers, clips shared by founders and LPs, and a clear picture of what your audience values.
04Day 90 · Review
Your decision

We will walk you through the results in detail: what grew, what did not, and what we would adjust. You decide whether to scale, refine or pause. The engagement is month to month throughout.

We do not forecast follower counts, because no one can do so reliably. What we commit to is full transparency on what we publish and what it tells us.
